3) Project Description
 The proposed solution, namely the Taylor’s Student Wellness and Nutrition App, is the digital tool that can help the Taylor’s University community maintain its physical and mental health directly related to SDG 3: Good Health and Well-Being.
 According to our initial study, a major section of the student population was extremely stressed, and lacked the ability to balance their diets in campus. This application fills that gap by offering:
 Nutritional transparency: Availability of menu and nutrition information of campus vendors.
 Mental Health Support: Stress-trackers and instant access to counseling information on the university.
 Community Integration: The options that assist students to locate wellness partners to go to the gym with or dine with them.
 4) Overview of SDLC (Fwaterfall Model)
 To achieve this, we have implemented the Waterfall Model in this project to have a well organised flow of the project with a well documented phase before proceeding to the next one.
 a) Requirements Gathering
 Primary survey was used to determine the pain points amongst the students of Taylor in this phase.
 Critical Result: More than three-quarters of the respondents identified their greatest challenge to maintaining a healthy lifestyle as Time Management and Campus Food Variety.
 Deliverable: A Software Requirements Specification (SRS) report describing such functional requirements as meal logging and stress monitoring.
 b) Analysis
 Analysis involved getting the raw data and converting it into logical models.
 Activity: Prepared Use Case Diagrams to describe the way in which a student will engage with the app as compared to an administrator (or vendor) to manage data.
 Goal: To make sure that the system architecture is capable of dealing with real time updates of cafeteria menu data.
 c) Design
 This stage is oriented on how of the system.
 UI/UX: It will be necessary to make low-fidelity wireframes and high-fidelity prototypes to make the interface user-friendly to busy students (Figma).
 System Design: This is where the database schema and class diagrams are designed in preparation of the coding stage.
 d) Implementation
 The "Coding" phase. The current process that our team is following is the modular design where we are developing both the front-end and back-end at the same time so that the core logic of the Wellness Tracker is well developed before we add social functionality.
 e) Testing/Feedback
 The app is thoroughly tested before the final delivery:
 Unit Testing: Determining individual features (such as the calorie calculator).
 User Acceptance Testing (UAT): Recruit a small sample of students at Taylor to go through the prototype and give their opinion of usability and relevance.
